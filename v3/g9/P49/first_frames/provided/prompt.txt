Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: photorealistic first-frame reference for an image-to-video low-Reynolds-number fluid benchmark

Primary request:
Create a single front-on viscometer-style laboratory photograph of one tall transparent rectangular tank filled with still glycerin. Inside the same liquid volume, suspend exactly two polished steel spheres made from the same material and density but with clearly different radii. The smaller sphere and larger sphere are side by side at exactly the same initial depth, fully submerged, far from every wall, and held by one shared synchronized release bar just beneath the liquid surface. Both are motionless before release, with a long unobstructed liquid column below them.

Scene/backdrop:
The tank is genuinely filled, not an empty glass box: show a straight liquid meniscus just below the top, a continuous pale honey-gold to colorless glycerin body extending to the bottom, and a subtle darkened liquid edge at the side walls. The glycerin should look optically clear but distinctly viscous and dense—smooth glassy transparency, faint warm amber tint, slightly thick rounded meniscus, and gentle refractive magnification around the submerged spheres. Keep the outside background plain cool gray and free of other objects.

Use a tall, slender rectangular tank whose inner liquid height is at least four times its inner width. Place both spheres side by side in the upper fifth of the liquid column at equal depth, with a very long, empty, clearly visible settling column below them. Keep generous horizontal separation between the two vertical paths and do not make the liquid column look shallow.

Give the steel spheres small soft specular highlights and a faint warm transmission gradient through the liquid. The waterline, side-wall refraction, and meniscus should be visible but understated; no caustic patterns, no waves, no blown-out reflections, and no contradictory shadow directions.

No suspended particles, bubbles, currents, cloudiness, or sediment.

Measurement-ready presentation:
The tank must be visibly tall and slender: inner liquid height at least four times the inner tank width, with the full top meniscus and bottom visible in the frame. Place both spheres in the upper fifth of the liquid column and leave at least eight largest-sphere diameters of unobstructed glycerin below them. Use a stable front-on scale, strong but natural sphere-to-liquid contrast, and clean empty paths so each sphere can be segmented and tracked independently.
